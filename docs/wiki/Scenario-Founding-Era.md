## Scenario Brief

**Era:** United States, 1787–1800  
**Setting:** A legislature being invented in real time. The procedural norms the other scenarios take for granted do not yet exist. Every vote is also a precedent.  
**Stakes:** Pass the foundational legislation of the republic: the Bill of Rights, the First Bank, the Judiciary Act, the Alien and Sedition Acts — and survive the first peaceful transfer of power in the history of modern democratic governance.

**Opening situation:** You enter the First or Second Congress with a chamber that is simultaneously larger than any previous American governing body and smaller than any modern one. Every senator knows every other senator. Relationships are everything. The faction system is proto-partisan: Federalist vs. Republican, with no party apparatus to enforce discipline.

**Central tensions:**
- Every bill is also a constitutional argument
- Federalist/Anti-Federalist split predates and outlasts the party system
- Slavery is a fault line that must be legislated around to maintain the union — and cannot be resolved without destroying it
- The precedents you set here will govern a country for centuries; the gameplay consequence is a "precedent" event flag system (planned)

**Named NPC archetypes planned:** Federalist Institution-Builder, Anti-Federalist Principled Dissenter, Southern Planter Pragmatist, Northeastern Merchant Moderate  
**Hooked systems:** Legislation (constitutional precedent layer), Factions (proto-party system), Events (Washington retirement, Adams-Jefferson succession), Congress (full procedural generation in 1780s context)

**Era register note:** elevated, classical, principled argument; no informality whatsoever; no anachronism. Characters argue from first principles, not from precedent — because there is no precedent.

**Victory framing (draft):**  
> *"The republic survives its first decade. The question of whether it deserved to is a matter for later historians."*

**Loss framing (draft):**  
> *"The convention fails. What comes next is not called a republic."*
