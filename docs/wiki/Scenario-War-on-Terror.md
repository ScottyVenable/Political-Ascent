## Scenario Brief

**Era:** United States, 2001–2010  
**Setting:** A legislature operating in permanent low-grade emergency after a catastrophic attack. The political cost of appearing soft on security is asymmetric. Every civil liberties bill is a liability until it is not.  
**Stakes:** Navigate the Authorization for Use of Military Force (AUMF), the USA PATRIOT Act renewals, the Iraq War authorisation, the financial crisis, and the first post-crisis recovery bills — a decade of consequential legislative moments with almost no legislative consensus.

**Opening situation:** You enter the chamber in the immediate aftermath of 9/11 or at a later entry point (player choice at scenario start). The emergency-urgency tone tag is the most powerful tool in the speech composer. The civil liberties and oversight factions are the most vulnerable to political pressure.

**Central tensions:**
- Security vs. liberty as the dominant bill axis
- War authorisation as a recurring mega-bill mechanic
- Financial crisis (2008) as a second-act set-piece event
- Public opinion is fragile; media-operator NPCs have outsized influence in this era

**Named NPC archetypes planned:** Post-9/11 Security Hawk, Civil Liberties Dissident, Bipartisan Crisis Pragmatist, Early Anti-War Principled Dissenter  
**Hooked systems:** Legislation (AUMF as mega-bill), Population (security fear as cohort attribute), Events (9/11 entry event, financial crisis arc), Media Operator archetype prominence

**Era register note:** compressed urgency, security-state dread; avoid self-congratulatory hindsight; the characters do not know how it ends.

**Victory framing (draft):**  
> *"The oversight bill passes. A surveillance program is constrained. Nobody thanks you for it."*

**Loss framing (draft):**  
> *"The vote was bipartisan. The consequences were not."*
