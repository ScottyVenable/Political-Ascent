## Scenario Brief

**Era:** United States, 1917–1919  
**Setting:** The country has just entered a European war it spent three years refusing. The domestic political environment is contracting: civil liberties are in the crossfire of war mobilisation politics. The Sedition Act is live legislation.  
**Stakes:** Manage war mobilisation funding, civil liberties legislation, and the domestic political fallout of a war that is wildly unpopular with specific cohorts — including large immigrant constituencies.

**Opening situation:** You enter a short and volatile legislative window. The war ends in approximately two years. The peace will be harder than the war. Your task is to shape the terms of American participation in both — and to keep your seat while doing it.

**Central tensions:**
- Civil liberties vs. war powers: every security measure is also a political weapon
- Labour and immigrant population cohorts are the primary victim populations of the war-powers bills — their happiness mechanics are under pressure
- Wilson White House as a powerful external actor (event layer)
- The Treaty of Versailles rejection is a set-piece end event (planned)

**Named NPC archetypes planned:** Progressive War Democrat, Isolationist Old Guard, Civil Liberties Principled Dissenter, Industrial War-Hawk  
**Hooked systems:** Legislation (war-powers bills), Population (immigrant cohort), Events (armistice, treaty)

**Era register note:** clipped, purposeful, duty-language; avoid nihilism or retroactive snark.

**Victory framing (draft):**  
> *"The armistice is signed. You kept the seat. The question of what it was worth is for later."*

**Loss framing (draft):**  
> *"The vote goes against you. History will not remember why you voted the way you did. It will remember the vote."*
