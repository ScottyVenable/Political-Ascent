## Scenario Brief

**Era:** United States, 1941–1945  
**Setting:** Total war mobilisation. The economy is being rebuilt for a single purpose. Every bill is a war bill at some degree of separation. The chamber is largely cooperative and entirely exhausted.  
**Stakes:** Pass mobilisation funding, manage post-war planning legislation, and shape the architecture of the post-war international order — the United Nations charter, the Marshall Plan framework, the GI Bill — all as in-run legislative arcs.

**Opening situation:** You enter the chamber four years before the war ends. The political coalition is uniquely broad and uniquely fragile: any deviation from the war consensus is a political catastrophe. But the decisions that will define the post-war world are being made now, in planning committees, by senators who are not thinking about post-war consequences.

**Central tensions:**
- Bipartisan coalition is stable on war, fractures immediately on anything else
- Post-war planning creates fissures between domestic-spending and international-engagement factions
- Japanese internment policy is a live ethical and legislative question in this period (event layer — planned with content sensitivity review)
- Race and civil rights as domestic fault lines under the surface of the war consensus

**Named NPC archetypes planned:** Bipartisan War Pragmatist, Southern Conservative Spoiler, Progressive Labour Democrat, Internationalist Republican  
**Hooked systems:** Economy (war mobilisation model), Legislation, Events (Pearl Harbor, D-Day, Hiroshima as set-piece events), Factions

**Era register note:** clipped, purposeful, duty-language; specific and concrete; no nihilism.

**Victory framing (draft):**  
> *"The war is won. The vote on the post-war order is tomorrow."*

**Loss framing (draft):**  
> *"The coalition didn't hold. The legislation didn't pass. The war is won by other means."*
