## Scenario Brief

**Era:** United States, 1929–1941  
**Setting:** The economy has collapsed. One in four Americans are unemployed. The question is not whether government should act — the question is whether it can act fast enough. The legislative clock runs against a real crisis.  
**Stakes:** Pass emergency economic legislation through a chamber that is simultaneously desperate for solutions and terrified of the precedents. The New Deal as a policy arc. The Supreme Court as an adversarial actor (planned event layer).

**Opening situation:** The economy indicator starts at crisis level across all seven metrics. The pressure to act generates both opportunity (emergency-urgency cards are more effective) and trap (coalition-destroying emergency measures are also on the table). Your first bill will be visible to everyone who is starving.

**Central tensions:**
- Legislative urgency is a real mechanic: bills drafted under crisis conditions have modified committee timelines
- Ideological fault lines: the New Deal coalition includes groups that deeply distrust each other
- Business faction and labour faction are in direct conflict; both need the player
- Constitutional constraints are event-driven barriers (Court-packing crisis planned as a set-piece event arc)

**Named NPC archetypes planned:** New Deal Pragmatist, Business-Aligned Reluctant Moderate, Labour Champion Crusader, Constitutional Dissenter  
**Hooked systems:** Economy (crisis mechanics), Events (Supreme Court arc), Legislation, Population (breadline happiness model)

**Era register note:** plain urgency, radio-broadcast cadence; no ironic detachment; characters speak with weight.

**Victory framing (draft):**  
> *"The act is signed. There are still nine million unemployed. You have three years."*

**Loss framing (draft):**  
> *"The relief bill dies in committee. The line outside the employment office gets longer."*
