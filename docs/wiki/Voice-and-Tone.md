# Voice and Tone

> **GDD reference:** §12.1 (Tone Bible), §12.2 (Voice & Style Guide)
> **Implementation status:** N/A (authoring guide)
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-05-02 (Vex, GDD v0.4-DRAFT)

This page is a player-facing distillation of the tone and voice conventions that govern all authored text in Political Ascent. It is written for contributors, wiki editors, and modders who want to write content that sounds like it belongs in the game.

---

## The Register

Political Ascent is **grounded political drama with a sardonic edge**. The game sits between the rhythm of a Sorkin walk-and-talk and the fatalistic realism of Crusader Kings III. Characters are capable and compromised. Institutions are real and imperfect. The player's choices feel consequential — not because the game manipulates their emotions, but because the stakes are always material.

The register is **sincere, not cynical.** Cynicism says the system is irredeemably broken. Political Ascent says the system is brutally difficult and worth fighting over. Satire is earned by specificity — a joke about committee procedure should reveal something true about committee procedure.

---

## What the Voice Sounds Like

**News headline:**
> *"Senate Panel Stalls Infrastructure Vote"*

Not: *"The Infrastructure Vote Has Been Stalled by the Senate Panel."*

**Event description (two or three sentences):**
> *"The energy bill clears committee on a party-line vote. The floor fight starts Monday. You have four days."*

Not: *"The energy bill has successfully passed through the committee phase and will now proceed to the floor debate stage."*

**Card flavour text (8–16 words, sharp image or wry observation):**
> *"Smile for the cameras. Shake every hand."*

> *"The check clears. The question of why it was written never does."*

Not: *"Use this card to gain Political Capital from donors."*

**Quest description (declarative, present tense):**
> *"The coalition is fracturing. Find common ground before the vote."*

Not: *"You must work to find common ground in order to complete this quest."*

## In Bounds and Out of Bounds

**In bounds:**
- Institutional friction as genuine obstacle, not punchline
- Personal moral compromise with visible costs
- Ambition as a legitimate and complex human drive
- Dry wit in flavour text that earns a half-smile without breaking immersion
- Tragedy as a valid arc resolution — a quest that ends in defeat can feel earned

**Out of bounds:**
- Real-person likenesses — no NPC names that map to living or recently-deceased political figures
- Partisan messaging — no text that reads as advocacy for a real-world party or ideology
- Nihilism — loss states must feel meaningful, not pointless
- Off-colour humour
- Fourth-wall breaks in in-world content
- Anachronistic idiom — a 1777 delegate does not speak in post-television vernacular

## Era Register

Each scenario has a voice calibration:

| Scenario era | Register | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Modern America 2024 | Compressed, media-saturated, self-aware | Purple prose, stilted formality |
| Cold War | Institutional gravity + subterranean paranoia | Slangy ironic detachment |
| Civil War & Reconstruction | Portentous, formal, weight of history | Casual contractions in formal addresses |
| Gilded Age | Florid public rhetoric masking private cynicism | Post-1960s idiom |
| Great Depression / New Deal | Plain urgency, radio-broadcast cadence | Detached irony |
| WWI / WWII | Clipped, purposeful, duty-language | Nihilism or retroactive snark |
| 9/11 / War on Terror | Compressed urgency, security-state dread | Self-congratulatory hindsight |
| Founding Era | Elevated, classical, principled argument | Any informality; anachronism |

## Capitalisation Rules (Quick Reference)

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Political Capital, Action Points | Title case — always |
| Bill titles | Title case — The Infrastructure Renewal Act |
| Trait names | Title case — Scandal Survivor |
| Faction names | Title case — Reform Bloc |
| Population cohort names | Title case — Working-Class Voters |
| Political office as title | Title case — Senator Reyes |
| Political office generic | Lowercase — "the senator voted" |

## Banned Phrasing

No "Based" (slang intensifier), no "Literally" (as intensifier), no "POV:", no social-media meta-language. These break immersion in every era.

Prefer the canonical terms over synonyms: **Political Capital** not "influence points," **Legislator** not "politician," **Bill stage** not "phase" or "status," **Passed / signed / failed / vetoed** not "approved" or "rejected."

---

## For Modders

If you are authoring events, cards, dialogue, or scenarios, your content will be evaluated against these conventions. Content that sounds like system documentation rather than in-world text will not be merged. Ask yourself: would a player read this and feel like they are in the chamber, or like they are reading a tooltip?
